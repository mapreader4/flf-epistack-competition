B  Inventory of earliest known covid cases

⟦1⟧ To be the best of my knowledge, there does not exist any publicly available list of the earliest
known covid cases. ⟦2⟧ The best source, and nearly the only source, on early covid cases is the
2021 WHO report [32] and its annex. ⟦3⟧ This report does not have a complete list of early
cases, ⟦4⟧ but does give aggregate data on known 2019 cases broken down by date and other
features, ⟦5⟧ as well as blurry maps of cases, which with some effort can be used to infer some
of the individual case information. ⟦6⟧ This aggregate data is derived from extensive searching
conducted by the WHO team, including interviews with many early patients, ⟦7⟧ which cannot
be independently verified or duplicated.
⟦8⟧ While the data in the WHO report is irreplaceable, it is not error free, ⟦9⟧ nor is it complete.
⟦10⟧ Notably other researchers have found that the WHO's primary case actually had on onset
of 2019 December 16, not 8; ⟦11⟧ and the report omits Wei Guixian with an onset of December
10, making her the primary covid case.
We begin by delineating the information in the WHO report, before incorporating other
sources. This separation is useful for several reasons:
• ⟦12⟧ I am not aware of any other place that presents the WHO data in tabular form.
• ⟦13⟧ The WHO report is the highest quality source, ⟦14⟧ so mixing other sources may introduce
quality issues.
• ⟦15⟧ It is relatively easy to understand the causes of ascertainment bias in the WHO report,
⟦16⟧ but once a diversity of sources are mixed it is much harder to control ascertainment
bias, ⟦17⟧ as even the selection of sources 75 can be a cause of bias.

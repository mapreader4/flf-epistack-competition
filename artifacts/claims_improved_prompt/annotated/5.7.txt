5.7  12 nucleotide insert at FCS

As shown in section 1.2.2, ⟦1⟧ sars-cov-2 has 12 extra nucleotides at the FCS when compared to
the most closely known bat viruses, such as banal-20-52.
⟦2⟧ Certainly this looks suspicious, but there is a lack of definite information to say anything
more than that. ⟦3⟧ There is no particular reason that this mutation could not have evolved by
chance. ⟦4⟧ The only thing odd about this insert is how large of a change it is compared to the
smaller changes elsewhere in the genome; however, ⟦5⟧ we do expect the S1/S2 boundary region
to be under high evolutionary pressure, especially as the virus crosses species into mammal
hosts that could make use of the FCS.
⟦6⟧ While banal-20-52 is the closest known virus, it is not close enough to make the kind of
comparison that Rootclaim seeks to do with sars-cov-2. ⟦7⟧ The two viruses are separated by
decades of evolutionary time and likely multiple recombination events with other viruses.

Claims:
⟦1⟧ SARS-CoV-2 has 12 extra nucleotides at the FCS when compared to the most closely known bat viruses, such as banal-20-52.
⟦2⟧ There is a lack of definite information to say anything more than that the 12 nucleotide insert looks suspicious.
⟦3⟧ There is no particular reason that the 12 nucleotide insert could not have evolved by chance.
⟦4⟧ The 12 nucleotide insert is a large change compared to the smaller changes elsewhere in the genome.
⟦5⟧ The S1/S2 boundary region is under high evolutionary pressure, especially as the virus crosses species into mammal hosts that could make use of the FCS.
⟦6⟧ Banal-20-52 is the closest known virus to SARS-CoV-2, but it is not close enough to make a comparison.
⟦7⟧ SARS-CoV-2 and banal-20-52 are separated by decades of evolutionary time and likely multiple recombination events with other viruses.

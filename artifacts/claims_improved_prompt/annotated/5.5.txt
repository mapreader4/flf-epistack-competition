5.5  ACE2 binding affinity

⟦1⟧ Piplani et al [28] found in computer simulations that sars-cov-2 binds best to human ACE2
receptors out of 14 species studied; ⟦2⟧ note that other studies found conflicting results.
This is hard to explain under zoonotic theory, ⟦3⟧ as the sars-cov-2 sample used was from 2019,
before significant evolution in human hosts should have happened. ⟦4⟧ However, the second
highest affinity studied was pangolin ACE2, which is similar to human ACE2; ⟦5⟧ if pangolins
were an intermediate host, then sars-cov-2 could have adapted to pangolin ACE2 and thus
partially to human ACE2.
⟦6⟧ Under the lab leak theory, this observation is explained as a result of lab workers screening
many different wild viruses to identify those that bind well to human ACE2, ⟦7⟧ and possible
then passaging the virus through mouse hosts that have been genetically modified to have
human ACE2 receptors. ⟦8⟧ While this may be a better explanation then the zoonotic theory,
it is still not a great explanation. ⟦9⟧ This process requires screening a large pool of wild viruses
to find one that happens to be well-adapted to humans already; ⟦10⟧ and presumably researchers
are screening to find which one best binds to humans, indifferent to whether it binds better
to humans than other animals. ⟦11⟧ Also, this screening process may use different simulations
than Piplani et al used.
⟦12⟧ A control is also required; maybe human ACE2, either in reality or in the simulation of
Piplani et al, is just particularly good at binding to spike proteins?
⟦13⟧ The high binding affinity found by Piplani et al appears to be weak circumstantial evidence
in favor of the lab leak hypothesis.

Claims:
⟦1⟧ Piplani et al found that sars-cov-2 binds best to human ACE2 receptors out of 14 species studied
⟦2⟧ Other studies found conflicting results regarding sars-cov-2 binding affinity
⟦3⟧ The sars-cov-2 sample used by Piplani et al was from 2019, before significant evolution in human hosts should have happened
⟦4⟧ The second highest affinity studied by Piplani et al was pangolin ACE2, which is similar to human ACE2
⟦5⟧ If pangolins were an intermediate host, then sars-cov-2 could have adapted to pangolin ACE2 and thus partially to human ACE2
⟦6⟧ Under the lab leak theory, the observation that sars-cov-2 binds well to human ACE2 is explained as a result of lab workers screening many different wild viruses
⟦7⟧ Lab workers may have passaged the virus through mouse hosts that have been genetically modified to have human ACE2 receptors
⟦8⟧ The lab leak theory may be a better explanation than the zoonotic theory for the observation that sars-cov-2 binds well to human ACE2
⟦9⟧ The process of screening many different wild viruses to find one that binds well to human ACE2 requires a large pool of wild viruses
⟦10⟧ Researchers screening wild viruses may be indifferent to whether the virus binds better to humans than other animals
⟦11⟧ The screening process used by lab workers may use different simulations than Piplani et al used
⟦12⟧ A control is required to understand the binding affinity of human ACE2 to spike proteins
⟦13⟧ The high binding affinity found by Piplani et al appears to be weak circumstantial evidence in favor of the lab leak hypothesis

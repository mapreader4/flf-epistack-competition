7  Analysis

Having criticized others' arguments at great length, at last I come to the more difficult task
of presenting my best guess as to the origin of the covid pandemic. I will present this in
the structure of a Bayesian inference computation, though not wholly quantitative, ⟦1⟧ with my
choice of numbers being subjective and uncertain.
LetZ be the hypothesis that covid emerged from a zoonotic spillover in HSM, and LL that
covid resulted from gain-of-function research in WIV; these are very slightly narrowings from
the actual hypotheses at stake.
Note that if you accept Rootclaim's argument in section 3.1, then ⟦2⟧ the definition I gave
for Z is quite a significant narrowing, ⟦3⟧ as they believe the observational evidence is equally
consistent with zoonotic spillover anywhere in Wuhan as in HSM, ⟦4⟧ and so Z would have a
suitably greater prior (but much lower posterior).
The first step is the most challenging, to estimate the priors P (Z) and P (LL). ⟦5⟧ These
numbers will be given as annual risk, so can also be interpretted as the yearly rate at which
pandemics emerge from those sources.

Claims:
⟦1⟧ The judge's choice of numbers in the Bayesian inference computation for the origin of the covid pandemic is subjective and uncertain.
⟦2⟧ The definition of Z (zoonotic spillover in HSM) is a significant narrowing of the actual hypothesis at stake.
⟦3⟧ Rootclaim argues that the observational evidence is equally consistent with zoonotic spillover anywhere in Wuhan as in HSM.
⟦4⟧ The prior probability of Z (zoonotic spillover in HSM) would be suitably greater if one accepts Rootclaim's argument in section 3.1, but its posterior probability would be much lower.
⟦5⟧ The judge will estimate the priors P(Z) and P(LL) as annual risk, which can also be interpreted as the yearly rate at which pandemics emerge from those sources.

A.5  Early mutation rate

⟦1⟧ ⟦2⟧ ⟦3⟧ ⟦4⟧ ⟦5⟧ Naively one expects that the mutation rate of a virus would be greatest immediately after
jumping species. ⟦6⟧ ⟦7⟧ ⟦8⟧ In principle this could be used to distinguish a zoonotic origin from a virus
that had been pre-adapted to a human host in the lab.
⟦9*⟧ ⟦10*⟧ In the proposal for Project DEFUSE, viruses would be evaluated based on disease they
cause in "humanized mice", ⟦11⟧ ie mice with human ACE2 receptors [14]. ⟦12⟧ While the result of
this filtering process would be better adapted to humans than wild viruses, ⟦13⟧ ⟦14⟧ ⟦15⟧ ⟦16⟧ ⟦17⟧ the effect would
presumably be confined to the receptor binding domain of sars-cov-2; ⟦18⟧ ⟦19⟧ the rest of the virus
would not be selected for being specialized for human hosts. ⟦20⟧ ⟦21⟧ ⟦22⟧ ⟦23⟧ ⟦24⟧ Therefore I do not expect the
early mutation rate to be useful in distinguishing the hypotheses without significant and
very technical investigation.

4.4  Early ascertainment bias

⟦1⟧ The ascertainment rate of covid refers to the fraction of covid cases that become known;
before covid became known, the main way for people to be diagnosed is retrospectively in
samples that were collected while they were in a hospital. ⟦2⟧ ⟦3⟧ People who became sick with covid
before 2020 and recovered without going to a hospital will mostly be unknown. ⟦4⟧ There is some
nuance here, in that people who visit local hospital clinics are less likely to have samples
amenable to retrospective analysis than those who went to a major tertiary hospital; and
the possible distinction between "going to a hospital" versus "being hospitalized". ⟦5⟧ ⟦6⟧ ⟦7⟧ ⟦8⟧ ⟦9⟧ We can
roughly estimate the early ascertainment rate as somewhere around 5% to 10% based on the
hospitalization rate of covid.
⟦10⟧ ⟦11⟧ ⟦12⟧ Ascertainment rate bias refers to any biases in how likely some cases are to be ascertained
than others; ⟦13⟧ ⟦14⟧ most notably that in the first half of 2020 January having an epidemiological
link to HSM was required for diagnosis. ⟦15⟧ Other, smaller biases likely also existed. However
⟦16⟧ ⟦17⟧ ⟦18⟧ I think the ascertainment rate for hospitalized cases to be quite good, ⟦19⟧ ⟦20⟧ and effectively zero
for non-hospitalized cases; if so, that places a sharp upper bound on how much bias could
exist.41

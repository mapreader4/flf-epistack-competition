1.3  Format of the debate

⟦1*⟧ ⟦2*⟧ Rootclaim was created to demonstrate the usefulness of a particular methodology for ana-
lyzing complicated questions, where there exists abundant lines of circumstantial evidence
both for and against any particular hypothesis. After systematically evaluating each piece
of evidence, they can be integrated into a gestalt that gives a final level of confidence we can
have in each hypothesis.
⟦3⟧ ⟦4⟧ I refer to this technique as Bayesian reasoning or Bayesian computation , which will be
discussed in the next section; however Rootclaim are far from the only ones to use Bayesian
reasoning, and I believe they claim to distinguish themselves from others in the details of
how the evaluate the evidence. In any case I agree with their critiques of other methodologies
(or, frequently, lack thereof) and that most people who get the right answer do so more by
chance than not.
This debate is the first organized by Rootclaim, of hopefully multiple (on different topics,
to be clear!), ⟦5⟧ as ⟦6⟧ a series of 3 spoken debate sessions ⟦7⟧ totalling 20 hours [1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6] ⟦8⟧ and
various written supplements [7, 8]. ⟦9⟧ I am one of two judges, deciding independently, ⟦10⟧ with the
outcome of the debate determined by the average of our decisions.
⟦11⟧ ⟦12⟧ I assess the debate structure designed by Rootclaim to be very fair and not create any
advantage for them, ⟦13⟧ and it was clear that Saar consciously acted in good faith throughout.
⟦14⟧ To me, the debate felt more like ⟦15⟧ a collaboration of four people working together to seek the
truth than an adversarial contest; it is rare to see contentious issues worked out in such a
productive manner between people acting in good faith.
⟦16⟧ ⟦17⟧ ⟦18⟧ As I find that both parties argued in good faith and there were no significant violations of (the

⟦21⟧ ⟦22⟧ Statements were evaluated independently of the credibility of other parties who assert them.

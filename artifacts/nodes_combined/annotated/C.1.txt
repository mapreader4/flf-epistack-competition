C.1  Anecdotal reports of the conditions at WIV

⟦1⟧ ⟦2⟧ Annex D7 of the WHO 2021 report presents second-hand information reported from members
of the WIV ([32], page 130 to 133 of annex). I will quote the relevant passages at length.
Professor Shi Zhengli (director of WIV Center for Emerging Infectious Diseases) was asked
about the laboratory work performed at WIV:
⟦3⟧ -⟦4⟧ her laboratory used recombinant viruses to test whether bat CoVs
could use ACE2 to bind but ⟦5⟧ used bat spike protein on a bat-CoV
backbone, not human SARS. ⟦6⟧ ⟦7⟧ ⟦8⟧ It is important to use this approach
because ⟦9⟧ it is difficult to isolate these viruses and envelope protein is
most important part to understand function. For example, other re-
searchers engineered SHC014 spike so did not need isolates for mouse
experiments. ⟦10⟧ ⟦11⟧ ⟦12⟧ ⟦13⟧ WIV began recombinant work in 2015 with WIV-1. ⟦14⟧ ⟦15⟧ It
received ACE2 mice in 2016 ⟦16⟧ and ⟦17⟧ started recombinant experiments
with WIV +SHC014 in 2018 ⟦18⟧ but did not finish them owing to the
COVID outbreak.
⟦19*⟧ Note how this compares to the work listed in the Project DEFUSE proposal, in which
chimeric viruses would install experimental spike proteins into non-pandemic virus back-
bones, and test them with mice bearing human ACE2 receptors.
⟦20⟧ The WIV director (Professor Yuan Zhiming?) discussed the possibility of sars-cov-2 escaping
from WIV:
⟦21⟧ ⟦22⟧ Staff had to report any symptoms every day after the outbreak of
COVID began. ⟦23⟧ ⟦24⟧ Serum samples were preserved annually for laboratory
staff. ⟦25⟧ There was extra testing during COVID outbreak according
to the Yuang Zhiming (laboratory director). ⟦26⟧ ⟦27⟧ The Institute did not
respond to conspiracy theories but understood why the WHO team
needed to ask. ⟦28⟧ ⟦29⟧ There had been no reports of unusual diseases, ⟦30⟧ none
diagnosed, and ⟦31⟧ all staff tested negative for SARS-CoV-2 antibodies.
⟦32*⟧ It is unclear to me if "Yuang Zhiming" is a typo, a different spelling of the same name, or
a different person. ⟦33⟧ ⟦34⟧ ⟦35⟧ ⟦36⟧ If it is true that all staff at WIV were tested and none had sars-cov-2
antibodies, that would make a lab leak unlikely.
Members of the WIV insist on proper adherence to laboratory safety:
⟦37⟧ ⟦38⟧ With regards to questions about laboratory workers, ⟦39⟧ ⟦40⟧ all underwent
a strict training regime that includes three levels with strict rules
on number of hours training and in-laboratory experience prior to
being allowed on own in lab, or to supervise others. ⟦41⟧ ⟦42⟧ P4 staff also
undergo psychological evaluation before being allowed to work in the
laboratory. ⟦43⟧ ⟦44⟧ ⟦45⟧ Physical and mental health was monitored; ⟦46⟧ no unusual
respiratory infections had been noted in the previous year. ⟦47*⟧ ⟦48*⟧ Good com-
pliance with mask use and hand hygiene was observed. ⟦49⟧ ⟦50⟧ ⟦51⟧ Surveillance
during the outbreak had been stringent; ⟦52⟧ no suspected or confirmed
case of COVID-19 was seen by PCR and antibody testing of all staff
was negative. ⟦53⟧ (If any worker had been infected, it would have been
likely that close contacts would have shown signs of infection.) ⟦54⟧ ⟦55⟧ Sera
were tested twice a year, and all had been negative. ⟦56⟧ ⟦57⟧ There had been
no turnover of staff in the coronavirus team.⟦58*⟧ 
⟦59*⟧ A rule of thumb I personally suggest when critically interpretting a passage such as the above
is to simply ignore so-called 'weasel'-words like "good" or "strict"; eg this yields "Compliance
with mask use and hand hygiene was observed. Surveillance during the outbreak existed...".
⟦60⟧ People, even when trying to twist things to their advantage, generally don't deliberately lie
about unambiguous things like whether an antibody test was positive or negative, but even
well-intentioned people will frequently imagine any training regime to be a strict training
regime if that is favorable to them. ⟦61⟧ It is also easy for vague qualifiers to be added as
information is passed along like a game of telephone; a first-hand account of a "training
regime" becomes a second-hand "strict training regime", etc.
⟦62⟧ ⟦63⟧ ⟦64⟧ Everyone believes they have high safety standards; what is important in the above are any
specific, tangible claims which are more likely than vague platitudes to have a basis in truth.
Yuan Zhiming denied the possibility of a lab leak from Wuhan:
⟦65⟧ ⟦66⟧ ⟦67⟧ ⟦68⟧ The rumours of a leak from the laboratory were refuted categorically
by the laboratory director for the following reasons:
• ⟦69⟧ among the three SARS-like viruses cultured in the laboratory,
⟦70⟧ none are closely related to SARS-CoV-2. ⟦71*⟧ ⟦72*⟧ The only SARS-CoV-
2-like virus found by this group is RaTG13, which is neither a
live (cultured) virus nor the progenitor of SARS-CoV-2
• [...] ⟦73⟧ ⟦74⟧ ⟦75⟧ ⟦76⟧ ⟦77⟧ ⟦78⟧ The reserved sera in April 2019 and March 2020 from all
the workers and students in research group led by Professor Shi
Zhengli were seronegative for SARS-CoV-2 antibodies.
• ⟦79⟧ ⟦80⟧ The laboratory director, responding to laboratory-leak theories,
commented that ⟦81⟧ from 2010, including the P3 laboratory, ⟦82⟧ WIV
has conducted experiments with more than 10,000 entries, ⟦83⟧ and
⟦84⟧ the P4 laboratory has conducted experiments with more than
3,000 entries in the last 3 years. ⟦85⟧ No infection was ever reported.
⟦86⟧ ⟦87⟧ ⟦88⟧ Close contacts would have been infected if there had been a
laboratory leak. But serum samples from Professor Shi's team
were all negative.
⟦89⟧ The second bullet point above is quite specific, and thus unlikely to be simple fabrication.
⟦90⟧ ⟦91⟧ However fabrication seems like the best explanation for how the text of the WHO report
could be compatible with a lab leak from the WIV; ⟦92⟧ other possibilities are a false negative
for antibodies, the screening omitted some lab workers, the primary case was not in Shi's
group, or perhaps some explanation I cannot think of.
⟦93⟧ Prior to the release of the WHO report, Peter Daszak gave a presentation on the WHO

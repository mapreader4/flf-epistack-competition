1.1  Background: what happened in Wuhan?

⟦1⟧ A butterfly with the flap of its wings is said to change the path of a hurricane on the other
side of the Earth. 3 There is no better exemplar of the butterfly effect than the beginnings
of a pandemic: ⟦2⟧ a single cough, or sneeze, a decision to go to work or see a friend, and the
lives of 10 million people swing in the balance.
⟦3⟧ Inevitably the earlier one tries to trace the origins of covid-19, the narrower the trail, until
it is reduced to the mundane daily activities of a handful of individuals whose identities may
be lost to time.
⟦4⟧ The farthest back we have been able to follow the trail is to the city of Wuhan, a massive
metropolis of 12 million people 4 in the Chinese province Hubei. ⟦5⟧ On 2019 December 26 or 27,
at the height of flu season,5 Dr Zhang Jixian6 examined two patients whose unusual CT scan
reminded her of her experience with the SARS-1 outbreak of 2002-2003; ⟦6⟧ she is generally
recognized as the discoverer of covid-19, thanks to her prompt and appropriate response.
⟦7⟧ Near this time, other healthcare workers began to notice a pattern of pneumonia cases and
likewise leapt to action.

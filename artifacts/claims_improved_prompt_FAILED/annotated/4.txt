You've just got home from a long day of hugging animals and taste-testing lab samples when
you realize you're feeling a bit under the weather. Congratulations: you are the primary
case28 in a new global pandemic! What happens next?
⟦1⟧ Most people in your position do not pass on their illness to others, 29 ⟦2⟧ but to result in a global
pandemic you would have to pass it on to one or more of your close contacts. ⟦3⟧ Depending on
your activities, you could easily have dozens or hundreds of potential transmissible contacts
over the course of your illness; a few will be household members or coworkers, but the
great majority often being strangers encountered in public. ⟦4⟧ Of course, such contacts are not
equally likely to acquire your illness.
⟦5⟧ To simplify matters, let us say you, and everyone else, have 100 potential contacts that do
not vary with time and are equally likely to become ill. 30 ⟦6⟧ We can build a massive graph
whose vertices are all the people in the world and edges represent close contacts. On this

Claims:
⟦1⟧ Most people in the position of being the primary case in a new global pandemic do not pass on their illness to others.
⟦2⟧ To result in a global pandemic, the primary case would have to pass the illness on to one or more of their close contacts.
⟦3⟧ The primary case could easily have dozens or hundreds of potential transmissible contacts over the course of their illness.
⟦4⟧ The primary case's contacts are not equally likely to acquire their illness.
⟦5⟧ For simplification, it is assumed that everyone has 100 potential contacts that do not vary with time and are equally likely to become ill.
⟦6⟧ A massive graph can be built with vertices representing all people in the world and edges representing close contacts.
⟦7*⟧ The graph can be used to track the path of a chain of disease transmission.
⟦8⟧ Each successive transmission acts as a random walk over the graph of close contacts.
⟦9⟧ After some interval of time, the disease is discovered in the index case.
⟦10⟧ The goal is to determine the probability distribution of who will be the index case for a particular hypothetical primary case.

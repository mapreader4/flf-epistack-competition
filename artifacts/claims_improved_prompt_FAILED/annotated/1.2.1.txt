⟦16⟧ Sars-cov-2 itself divides into many subgroups; ⟦17⟧ like the flu virus, one often finds that newer
variants displace older variants, causing notational difficulties as the phylogenetic tree divides
further and further. ⟦18⟧ It was for this reason that the PANGO notation was developed [29];
⟦19⟧ for example, the pango notation "B.1.1.529" refers to the root of the omicron variant. ⟦20⟧ The
very earliest division of the sars-cov-2 phylogeny is into two parts, called lineages A and B
in the PANGO notation.12 ⟦21⟧ Lineage B would contain the highly successful D614G mutation,
⟦22⟧ meaning that the aspartic acid (D) at location 614 of the spike protein (70 nucleotides from
the FCS) is replaced by glycine (G). ⟦23⟧ The D614G mutation first became prominent in Italy
in 2020 March, ⟦24⟧ and I believe is directly related to the worldwide March-May peak many
countries observed.13 ⟦25⟧ Lineage B has now completely displaced lineage A.
12⟦26⟧ In Nextstrain, lineage A is called 19B, as it was the second branch to be identified in 2019, and the
root of lineage B is called 19A; the root of omicron is called 21M. ⟦27⟧ In GISAID, lineage A is called S clade.
⟦28⟧ Nextstrain and GISAID provide freely available tools for viewing phylogenetic trees, tracking variants, and
downloading genomes.
13⟦29⟧ In summer of 2020 I invested some time into tracking the progression of D614G, but never finished my
work ⟦30⟧ as it was eventually obviated by the second wave that came that fall.
⟦31⟧ Due to the high mutation rate of viruses, a phylogenetic tree can be created with some
reliability down to the level of individual illnesses. ⟦32⟧ Samples of sars-cov-2 collected in 2019
December and 2020 January have been sequenced and some are available in GISAID, ⟦33*⟧ facil-
litating assembling them into a phylogenetic tree of the very earliest covid cases, though the
results are not without dispute. ⟦34*⟧ Lineages A and B each clearly form a tree with a single un-
ambiguous root from which there are many branches, called a "polytomy". ⟦35⟧ These two roots
differ from each other in exactly two nucleotides. ⟦36⟧ The root of lineage A has the sequence
T8782, C28144 (abbreviated T/C) ⟦37⟧ and the root of lineage B has the sequence C8782, T28144
(abbreviated C/T); ⟦38⟧ note that the mutation at 8782 is synonymous. ⟦39⟧ Several early samples
have been collected that may exhibit the C/C or T/T pattern. ⟦40⟧ The phylogenetic relationship
between the roots of the A and B lineages is not immediately clear without further analysis.

• Lineage B
• alpha, beta, . . . , omicron
• merbecoviruses (aka Lineage C)
• MERS
